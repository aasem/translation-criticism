Literary and scientific translation into Urdu

Translation is an art of failure.
Let's judge the error surface.

Urdu is seeing a boom in literary translation, and since generative AI arrived, more of it is made at speed. Machine assistance does not in itself cheapen a literary act, but translation is not transcription. It asks for diligent reading and interpretation, with a feel for register, image and rhythm in two languages at once. None of this can be hurried.

Publishers rarely have the means to judge a translation, and common readers have none at all. As AI-assisted translations multiply, the work of telling the careful from the merely fluent, and both from the hack job, has fallen far behind.

These assessments are an attempt to close that gap. The process is semi-automated:

  1. A sample is segmented sentence by sentence and set against its source, and potential deviations are flagged automatically as a first pass.
  2. I then read every segment myself and record the deviations I find.
  3. These deviations are scored on a framework adapted from Multidimensional Quality Metrics.
  4. On that analysis I give my verdict, and I own it.

The verdict is an informal editorial decision for drafts and a grade for books in print. The assessments are offered as a public service: to readers deciding what to trust, to publishers deciding what to print, and to translators who want their work read with the care it asks for.

If you want to get your translation graded, or disagree with a verdict or the review behind it, please reach me at asembuxi [at] gmail [dot] com.
